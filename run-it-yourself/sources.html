Reasons and sources

Why every odd is what it is, the sources behind it, and how the model changed from version 1 to the locked version 4. Where a number here and the code differ, the code and the odds table are right.

The forksVersion history

Model: the code. One Cold War is every documented crisis, war scare, false alarm and nuclear accident from July 1945 to the end of the USSR on 26 Dec 1991, in real order. At each one the dice decide whether it stays a near-miss (as it really did) or goes the other way.

Outcomes

  • NUCLEAR WW3: the superpowers at nuclear war. The run ends there.
  • CONVENTIONAL WW3: a superpower war that stays non-nuclear. The run ends, because the Cold War is over in another way.
  • LIMITED NUCLEAR USE: weapons used in a smaller war with no superpower exchange. The run goes on, but the taboo is broken: every later first step is ×1.25 / ×1.5 / ×2 (Tannenwald, The Nuclear Taboo, 2007).
  • SURVIVED: the run reaches 26 Dec 1991.

The odds are my judgements, written down openly. No source gives these exact numbers.

The three columns

  • Safe follows the sceptics: Tertrais, "On the Brink? Really?", Washington Quarterly 40(2), 2017. He studied 37 crises and 12 technical incidents and found most were "not-so-close" to "very distant".
  • Middle is the headline.
  • Dangerous follows the alarm view: Lewis, Williams, Pelopidas and Aghlani, Too Close for Comfort, Chatham House 2014 (13 near-uses 1962–2002). Also Lundgren, "What Are the Odds?", Nonproliferation Review 20(2), 2013 (an annual risk above 1%).

How the middle column compares with published estimates

  • Hellman (2008; IEEE Spectrum) puts the risk around 1% a year, and more than 10% for the Cuban crisis alone.
  • Kennedy put the odds of war in Cuba at "somewhere between one out of three and even" (as reported in R. Kennedy, Thirteen Days; Allison, Foreign Affairs 2012).
  • The model's middle gives about 14% for Cuba (nuclear WW3, all forks) and about 29% for the whole Cold War. That is below Lundgren and close to Hellman.

Incident list: Baum, de Neufville and Barrett, A Model for the Probability of Nuclear War, GCRI Working Paper 18-1, 2018 (60 incidents, in 00_research/sources). Every event below comes from it, except where marked.

Warheads at each date: Norris & Kristensen, "Global nuclear weapons inventories, 1945–2010", Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists 2010. Version history

v1 (30 Oct 2026): 712,099 of 1,000,000 survived (71.2%); "nuclear WW3" 251,882. Kept as sim_v1.mjs and RESULTS_v1.keep.md.

v2 (30 Oct 2026): an audit against every mistake class from the seven WW2 versions.

  1. A label that inflated the answer (the WW2 v1 class). Before 1955 the USSR had under 200 warheads, so a superpower nuclear war then is a one-sided ATOMIC WAR, not the end of the world. It is now its own outcome, and the headline counts only NUCLEAR WW3.
  2. Two odds for one war (the WW2 v3 class). In v1 a war starting from the U-2 shootdown had its own 50% nuclear odds, while the invasion fork had 35%. Every road into an invasion of Cuba now uses the same dice, drawn once.
  3. A danger switched off by another branch (the WW2 v4/v5 class). In v1 the B-59 danger vanished once an invasion began. The submarines are now hunted in both worlds.
  4. Odds leaning dramatic (the WW2 v3 class). These follow what actually happened:
  5. Berlin 1948 lowered from 3% to 2%: no shot was fired at the airlift.
  6. Korea 1950 lowered from 7% to 5%: no bombs were sent to Korea in 1950.
  7. Preventive war lowered from 1% to 0.5%.
  8. New tests (the WW2 v7 class), all passing:
  9. Real history survives in all three columns.
  10. Fixed dice per world.
  11. The paired test: forcing any fork the dangerous way never saves a world (20,000 worlds × 38 forks). This test caught bug 3.
  12. The middle annual hazard is about 0.59%/yr, between the sceptics and Lundgren's "greater than 1%".
  13. Cuba ends 10.9% of worlds: under Kennedy's 1-in-3 odds of war, near Hellman's ">10%".

Changed odds and mechanics (each with its evidence):

  • A superpower nuclear exchange that stops after a few weapons: now 15% / 10% / 5% (was 25% / 15% / 5%). Proud Prophet, the top-level US war game of June 1983: every road, from a demonstration shot to limited strikes, ended in all-out war (Bracken).
  • Cuba invasion goes nuclear: now 20% / 40% / 65% (was 15% / 35% / 60%).
  • For: the National Security Archive says tactical weapons "would most definitely be used in a first salvo" if US troops had landed.
  • Against: Malinovsky's order of 27 Oct 1962 forbade any nuclear use without orders from Moscow.
  • After an invasion that stays non-nuclear, Moscow answers in Berlin: 5% / 15% / 30%, with the Berlin 1961 nuclear odds. This was Kennedy's own fear.
  • U-2 retaliation: now 8% / 20% / 40% (was 5% / 15% / 30%). The ExComm had agreed in advance to strike the SAM site if a U-2 was downed, then held back.
  • Korea, the Soviets dragged in: now 5% / 15% / 30% (was 10% / 20% / 35%). Stalin kept his airmen's role secret and avoided open war.
  • Labels corrected:
  • The "2,200 missiles" call that had Brzezinski about to wake Carter is the 9 Nov 1979 training tape (National Security Archive, EBB 371).
  • The June 1980 46-cent chip filled the screens with 2s (200, 2,000).
  • The Korea 1951 event is the transfer of atomic cores to Guam (number not stated).

New outputs and tests:

  • Quiet worlds: how many Cold Wars were as quiet as ours, with no nuclear weapon used in any war.
  • Test 8: our history must be a common result under the middle odds (at least 30%; it is about 50%).
  • "Secret incidents" sensitivity: every alarm and accident doubled, because the known list is mostly US-declassified (Pelopidas).

v4 (30 Oct 2026): the full second-opinion review.

Every fork that ends at least 0.2% of worlds (24 events, 90% of all endings) was argued from both sides. See REVIEW_v4.md.

Changes:

  • New event: B-36, B-130 and B-4. These were the other Cuban submarines with nuclear torpedoes, also forced up (NSA EBB 75). Odds 0.2% / 1% / 5% fire, lower than B-59 because they were hunted after the 28 Oct deal.
  • Iran 1946 war: 0.5% → 0.3%. Truman's "ultimatum" never existed.
  • Dien Bien Phu use: 3% → 2%. Britain refused to join.
  • Sino-Soviet 1969 use: 4% → 3%. Most scholars read Moscow's soundings as coercion.
  • Able Archer: believed 8% → 5%, launch 12% → 10%. Miles 2020 (Warsaw Pact archives) weighed against PFIAB 1990.
  • 1980 chip: 0.5% → 0.3%.
  • Gulf War spread: 2% → 0.5%.

Kept after review: Cuba (strikes 25%, nuclear if invaded 40%), B-59 (5%), Berlin 1961 (2%), Petrov (50% × 4%), Taiwan, the NORAD tape.
